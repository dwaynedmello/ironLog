Budget

€45 to €60 a week, about €6.50 to €8.50 a day. Chicken is roughly half of it, so that is the only line worth touching if the number needs to come down.

Prices are crowd-reported estimates, not your branch's shelf. Write down what you actually pay on the first shop.

Breast or thigh?

The cheap thigh packs are a trap, and it is bone. Schenkel and Oberschenkel are sold bone-in and skin-on. Bone is 25 to 30% of the weight and skin another 10 to 15%, so you keep roughly half to 60% of what you pay for.

When it is worth it

Only if you find boneless skinless thigh fillets (ohne Knochen, ohne Haut) under about €7/kg. Then it is a genuine saving, and thigh tastes better and survives reheating, which counts when you eat it 14 times a week.

The two unfamiliar ones

Rote Linsen · red lentils

Dry pulses in a 500 g paper or plastic pack, in the Hülsenfrüchte aisle next to the rice and dried beans. Not a tin, not frozen. They look like small orange split discs.

It has to be the red ones. This is the whole reason the one-pot method works:

TypeCooking time
Rote Linsen10–15 min, no soaking
Beluga / grüne Linsen25–30 min
Braune Tellerlinsen45–60 min, soak first

White rice takes 10 to 12 minutes. Red lentils match it almost exactly, so they go in the same pot at the same time. Brown Tellerlinsen take four to five times as long and would need soaking the night before, which defeats the point entirely.

Three at Kaufland, all 500 g: K Classic rote Linsen €1.29, Baktat €1.59, K-Bio rote Linsen €1.65. You use 350 g a week, so a pack lasts about ten days. Since cost is not your constraint, take the Bio one: it is 36 cents more.

Red lentils are hulled and split, which removes some of the fibre that whole brown lentils keep. Even on a conservative figure the swap still lands you near 30 g of fibre a day, so the case holds, but read the Ballaststoffe line on your pack.

Or tinned, from the Konserven aisle

Pre-cooked lentils in a tin also work, and the cooking-time problem disappears, so brown is fine in a tin. Use 100 g drained in place of 25 g dry.

Per mealkcalProteinFibre
25 g dry red886.2 g2.0 g

Tinned actually gives more fibre, because they are whole browns rather than hulled reds. Bonduelle at €1.79 has three ingredients: water, lentils, salt. K-Classic braune Linsen at €0.69 is the same thing cheaper. Avoid "Linsen mit Suppengrün", which has vegetables mixed in so you cannot weigh it.

The catch is salt, about 0.56 g per 100 g, so roughly 1.1 g a day. Rinse them. And five tins a week costs more than one bag of dry.

Prinzessbohnen · green beans

Freezer aisle, 1 kg bag. Prinzessbohnen are the thin tender ones, which is what you want. Brechbohnen are the same bean cut into chunks and are coarser. Either works, but Prinzessbohnen are nicer.

K Classic do a 1 kg frozen bag, last listed at €1.99, though that listing is flagged as expired so check the shelf. Straight from frozen into the microwave like the broccoli, 4 to 5 minutes.

Avoid the tinned ones (Konserven). They are softer, saltier, and there is no reason to prefer them here.

Buying notes

  • Broccoli frozen, not fresh. Cheaper, no trimming waste, no spoilage, freezer to microwave. Nutritionally a wash.
  • Chicken: plain fillets. The own-brand ones are often sold leicht gewürzt, pre-seasoned. You want unseasoned.
  • Skyr at 250 g, or Magerquark at 200 g. Skyr is 10 g protein per 100 g against quark's 13.5, so it needs the bigger portion to match. It earns that by staying creamy in the oats instead of setting solid. Cremig is not higher fat, it is still 0.2 g. Not German griechischer Joghurt, which is ~10% fat.
  • Not Naturjoghurt. Only 5.8 g protein per 100 g. It would cost you ~9 g of protein a day.
  • Oil lasts 3 to 4 weeks, so it is not a weekly buy.
  • 20 eggs buys 14 used plus 6 spare. 6 chicken trays gives 200 g spare.

Why these actually help

Fibre is the measurable one

You were at roughly 24 g a day. The lentils and the mixed vegetables take you to about 29 g, against a 30 g reference intake. That is a real, countable improvement and it is the single clearest gain here.

Broccoli has a blind spot

Broccoli is excellent for vitamin C, vitamin K, folate and sulforaphane. What it has very little of is beta-carotene. Carrots and red peppers supply that. Eating one vegetable fourteen times a week means whatever that vegetable lacks, you lack too, every single day.

Red pepper also carries 128 mg of vitamin C per 100 g, which is more than broccoli, so the rotation is not a trade-down on the thing broccoli is famous for.

Plant variety, with the caveat

The American Gut Project found that people eating more than 30 different plant foods a week had a more diverse gut microbiome than those eating 10 or fewer. Your rotation plus oats, banana, almonds, rice and lentils puts you around 10 plant types, which is a meaningful move in that direction.

But be careful how much weight you put on that. The World Cancer Research Fund, which publicises the finding, explicitly states that eating 30 plants a week is not one of their recommendations, and the onward link to bowel cancer risk is their extrapolation rather than something the study measured. Treat it as suggestive, not established.

The olive oil

Extra virgin is cold-pressed and keeps the polyphenols and flavour compounds that refining strips out. That is a difference in what is in the bottle rather than a health claim I am going to make for you. At 140 g a week the better bottle costs you almost nothing.

What I could not verify

Whether organic or free-range chicken is nutritionally better. I tried to read the Reading University paper on fat and fatty acid composition and a sensory study on whether tasters can tell them apart, and both were blocked. Freiland and Bio reliably buy welfare and usually firmer texture from slower-growing birds. I am not going to claim more than that.

Day

~2,130 kcal~176 P · 59 F · ~232 C

Every weight is raw or dry. Rice roughly triples cooked and chicken loses about a quarter. A cooked-weight mistake is worth hundreds of calories a day.

The dial is the rice. Weigh yourself daily, look once every three weeks. Losing slower than 0.37 kg a week, drop to 60 g. Faster, go to 90 g. Change nothing else.

Sunday batch · 40 min

Do this once and the week cooks itself.

  • Chicken. 6 portions of 200 g on a tray, oven 200°C fan, 20 to 22 min. Season dry before it goes in. Cool, fridge, use within 3 days and freeze the rest.
  • Oats. 3 or 4 jars: 70 g oats + 250 g Skyr in each. Stir, lid on, fridge. No milk or water needed, Skyr is already loose enough.
  • Eggs. Boil 6 at once. 7 min for jammy, 9 for firm. Straight into cold water, then fridge in the shell.
  • Lentils, if you bought dry. Nothing to do now, they cook with the rice.
  • Not the rice. See the warning below.
Rice is the one thing you do not batch casually. The Bacillus cereus toxin is heat resistant, so reheating does not rescue rice that sat out. Cook it fresh daily, or cool it in a shallow tray within 90 minutes, fridge below 5°C, and reheat once only.

Breakfast · 2 min

Take a jar out of the fridge. Slice in 120 g of banana, scatter 25 g of almonds on top, and eat it with 2 boiled eggs on the side.

Skyr stays creamy overnight and needs no thinning. That is the main reason to prefer it over Magerquark here, which sets firm and crumbly and has to be loosened with water.

Lunch & dinner · 10 min

  • Rice and lentils together, if dry. 50 g rice + 25 g red lentils, 175 ml water, lid on, 10 to 12 min simmer then 5 min off the heat. In from cold, same pot, no separate step. This only works with rote Linsen.
  • Or tinned lentils. Drain 100 g into a sieve and rinse under the tap for a few seconds, which washes off a good part of the salt. Stir through the hot rice at the end. They are already cooked, so they need no time at all. Cook the rice alone at 50 g in that case.
  • Broccoli. 200 g from frozen, microwave 4 to 5 min with a splash of water. No defrosting, no chopping.
  • Chicken. Already cooked. 40 seconds in the microwave, or eat it cold, which is faster and perfectly fine.
  • Vegetable. 200 g of whatever the day calls for. Frozen goes straight in the microwave, peppers and carrots get chopped.
  • Oil last. Drizzle the weighed 10 g over the finished plate.
Do not fry in the 10 g of oil. A good chunk stays in the pan, so you eat maybe 6 g and count 10. Cook the chicken dry, then add the oil to the plate. Same number on paper, but this way you actually get the fat the plan depends on.

Flavour · five rotations

All of these are effectively free. Mix into the rice or over the chicken.

  • Soy & ginger. Soy sauce, grated garlic, grated ginger, chilli flakes.
  • Lemon & herb. Lemon juice, black pepper, dried oregano, garlic powder.
  • Smoky. Smoked paprika, cumin, garlic powder, a little salt.
  • Mustard & dill. Dijon, white wine vinegar, dried dill. Good cold.
  • Curry. Curry powder bloomed in the hot rice, with a spoon of the vegetable water to loosen it. Works especially well once the lentils are in.

Creamy sauces, shop-bought marinades and oil-based dressings are where a carefully weighed diet quietly falls apart. If you cannot weigh it, assume it is more than you think.

Making it better

  • Broccoli is nearly free at 34 kcal per 100 g. Doubling to 400 g costs 68 kcal. When a day feels hard, more vegetables is almost always the cheapest answer.
  • Rotate the vegetable a few times a week. One vegetable daily is one narrow slice of micronutrients. Peppers, carrots, spinach.
  • Fibre is the weak spot. White rice has 1.3 g per 100 g. Swapping part of it for brown rice, or adding a tin of lentils, fixes it and adds protein.
  • Your supplements cover the fish. Check the EPA + DHA figure on the tub, not the total oil. A 1000 mg capsule usually contains a fraction of that as the thing you actually want.
